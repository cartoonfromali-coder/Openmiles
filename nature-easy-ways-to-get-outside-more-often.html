Easy Ways to Get Outside More Often

Spending more time outdoors does not have to mean planning a long walk or setting aside an entire day. Small changes can make outdoor time easier to fit into everyday life.

Start With Short Breaks

You do not need a large block of free time to step outside. A short walk around the neighbourhood, a few minutes in a garden, or simply sitting outdoors can provide a useful change from being inside.

Short breaks are often easier to repeat because they require less preparation and can fit around existing routines.

Attach Outdoor Time to Something You Already Do

One of the easiest ways to build a new habit is to connect it with something familiar. You could take a short walk after lunch, spend part of your morning outside, or choose to walk a slightly longer route when running an everyday errand.

The goal is to make going outside feel like part of the day rather than another task that needs to be scheduled.

Make the Outdoors Convenient

Keep useful outdoor items easy to reach. Comfortable shoes, a light layer, or a small bag can remove some of the friction that makes quick outdoor plans feel like effort.

When the practical details are simple, it becomes easier to make a decision to step outside.

Explore Familiar Places Differently

You do not always need to travel somewhere new. A familiar park or local street can feel different when you change your route, visit at another time of day, or pay more attention to the surroundings.

Small changes in perspective can make nearby places more interesting.

Keep It Flexible

Outdoor time works best when it can adapt to the rest of your day. Some days might allow for a longer walk, while others may only leave room for a few quiet minutes outside.

There is value in both. Making outdoor time flexible and realistic gives you a better chance of keeping it as part of your normal routine.
